## What role do birds play in nature?

Imagine a morning without birdsong: it would be pretty quiet. 

Birds aren’t just fascinating because of their songs; they also play a central role in **ecosystems**. Here are a few examples:

**Seed dispersal:** Many bird species contribute to the spread of plants. They eat fruits and then excrete seeds elsewhere or hide them. In this way, birds connect **habitats** and support the development of forests and meadows. If an important bird species is missing, some plants spread much less effectively.

**Pollination of flowers:** It’s not just bees; some bird species also pollinate flowers. In the tropics, for example, hummingbirds carry **pollen** from flower to flower. Plants and birds are often closely dependent on one another in this process.

**Natural pest control:** Many birds eat large quantities of insects, spiders, and other arthropods. During the breeding season, a pair of blue tits brings many insects to their chicks every day. In this way, birds keep **pests** such as aphids or mosquitoes in check and support agriculture and gardens without the use of chemicals.

**Environmental bioindicators:** Birds often serve as nature’s **“fever thermometer”**, as they are vital indicators of the health of our environment. Since they inhabit nearly all of the world’s ecosystems and niches, changes in their populations and distribution can signal far-reaching shifts in biodiversity as a whole. If certain bird species suddenly become rarer or disappear entirely from a forest, something is wrong there—the natural balance has been disrupted. Because many birds react very quickly to environmental changes, we can detect early on whether an ecosystem is deteriorating or recovering. Bird conservation is therefore also **human conservation**, because what harms birds often points to problems that ultimately affect us all.

## What is the difference between songs and calls?

Birds use two types of sounds: **calls** and **songs**.

**Calls** are usually short, simple, and serve a clear purpose, such as sounding an alarm, maintaining contact, or demanding food. They are used throughout the year.

**Songs** are longer, often complex sequences of sounds. In many species, it is primarily the males that sing, but in some species, the females do as well. Especially during the **breeding season**, songs serve to mark the **territory** or attract mates.

## Why do birds sing?

In biology, the **acoustic communication** of birds plays a central role. Calls and songs serve vital purposes: finding a mate (courtship songs), signaling danger (alarm calls), and coordinating group behavior, such as during foraging.

This complexity is particularly evident in subtle differences:

- **Dialects and call types:** Many species have regional dialects, which can make identification difficult.
- **Life stages:** Even within the same species and at the same location, adult birds and juveniles can often be distinguished by their vocalizations.
