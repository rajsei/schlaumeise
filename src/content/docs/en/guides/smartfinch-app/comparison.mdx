Smartfinch grew out of **BirdNET Live**.
Under the hood, both apps use the same technology: the **BirdNET+ AI**, which recognizes animal sounds directly on the phone, and the **geo model**, which knows which species occur where and when.
What the two apps do with that technology, however, is fundamentally different.

<Columns min="18rem">
<div>

### Smartfinch – the collecting game

Smartfinch is made for **children aged 8 to 13** – and for the adults who accompany them.
There is only one mode: Live.
Every find earns stars, new species go into the collection, and everything is explained so that a child understands it.

</div>
<div>

### BirdNET Live – the research tool

BirdNET Live is made for people who need **reliable acoustic records**: in nature conservation, research, surveys and citizen science projects.
The app offers several recording modes, many settings for the AI, and exports the data in scientific formats.

</div>
</Columns>

## The differences at a glance

| | **Smartfinch** | **BirdNET Live** |
|---|---|---|
| **For whom?** | Children aged 8 to 13, families, school classes | Field researchers, conservation, citizen science, bird enthusiasts |
| **Goal** | Getting to know bird songs through play | Recording and documenting animal sounds reliably |
| **Modes** | one mode: **Live** | Live, Point Count, Survey, ARU, File Analysis |
| **Rewards** | Stars, collection, badges, achievements, levels, stickers | no game elements |
| **Results** | Journal by day, one day as a picture to share | Sessions with metadata, export as Raven Pro, CSV, JSON, GPX or ZIP |
| **Settings** | a simple first page, everything else under "Advanced"; no expert AI options | full control over the AI: window duration, confidence, sensitivity, inference rate |
| **Location** | only as a coarse area; decides what an animal is worth | GPS route in Survey mode, location of every detection |
| **In the background** | optional, stops by itself after 15 to 120 minutes | for hours or days in Survey and ARU mode |
| **Scientific claim** | none – a detection is the AI's guess | data for monitoring and surveys |
| **Language of the app** | child-friendly, no jargon | technical, with terms such as session, survey or inference |
| **Availability** | not yet in the stores, source code on GitHub | Google Play and App Store |

**What both apps have in common:** they are free and open source, work without the internet, upload no recordings and recognize almost 9,800 animal species – birds, mammals, amphibians and insects.

## Why two apps?

A game and a research tool sometimes need exactly the opposite.
Three examples show why Smartfinch is not simply BirdNET Live with stars:

<NumberedList>

1. **Long recordings**

    In BirdNET Live it makes sense to leave the phone in the woods for days as a recording unit (ARU). In Smartfinch, each animal earns stars **only once a day**. Leaving the phone running for hours therefore achieves almost nothing – going somewhere new achieves a lot. Curiosity is rewarded, not running time.
2. **How sure the AI is**

    In BirdNET Live you may lower the confidence threshold to find quiet or distant calls too. In a game, that would be the shortest path to a collection full of birds that were never there. So in Smartfinch the rule is: **higher is always allowed, lower means no stars.** Below 35 %, the app switches to test mode.
3. **The data**

    BirdNET Live stores metadata with every detection and exports it for analysis on a computer. Smartfinch shares only a single picture per day – with the date, stars and species, but **without recordings, location or place names**. For children, that is the safer choice.

</NumberedList>

## Which app suits me?

<Columns min="18rem">
<div>

### Choose Smartfinch if …

- you are between 8 and 13 years old,
- you want to discover birds with children,
- you want to get to know bird songs through play,
- you want to listen outside regularly and watch your collection grow,
- you want to use the app in primary school or the lower grades of secondary school.

</div>
<div>

### Choose BirdNET Live if …

- you want to record birds in a structured way, for example in a survey,
- you want to export your results and analyse them further,
- you want to use your phone as a recording unit in the field,
- you want to analyse existing audio files,
- you are working on a research project with older students.

</div>
</Columns>

## From Smartfinch to BirdNET Live

The two apps work well one after the other.
Anyone who has learned their first forty bird songs with Smartfinch and wants to know more will find the right tool in BirdNET Live: point counts at a fixed location, routes with GPS or recordings over several days.
Recognition works just the same – only the goals get bigger.

Both apps can be installed side by side on the same phone.
They don't share any data, though: the collection in Smartfinch and the sessions in BirdNET Live stay separate.

## Tip for teachers

For a first introduction in primary school or in grades 5 to 7, Smartfinch is a good fit: the children learn to listen closely, to tell species apart and to understand why a bird is rarer in winter than in summer.
From the middle grades or in project weeks, BirdNET Live can be added, for example for a bird survey on the school grounds.
If you want to build your own recording station, you will find the right material among the [projects](../../../projects/project02/).
