With a few simple tricks, Smartfinch recognizes considerably more birds.
For most of them you don't even need to touch the settings – the defaults suit almost everyone.

## Tips for better detections

- **Be quiet.** Conversations and other loud noises drown out quiet calls. In the spectrogram you can see your voice covering the bird.
- **Watch out for the wind.** Wind on the microphone makes a rushing noise. Stand so that your body shields the phone from the wind.
- **Get closer.** Distant calls lose their high notes. The app then sometimes doesn't recognize them, even though you can still hear them.
- **Use a better microphone.** An external microphone – wired or Bluetooth – hears further and more clearly than the built-in one. Point it to where the bird is singing.
- **Take power with you.** Listening uses a lot of battery. For long trips a power bank is worth it.
- **Check what the AI says.** Even when the app is very sure, it can be wrong. Listen to the recording before you believe it – especially with rare birds.

## The settings

You open the settings with the **Settings** tile on the home screen.
The first page holds everything children and parents really need. Everything else is one page further down under **Advanced settings**.

### The first page

**General** – Language, a light or dark design and a colour scheme for the app, for example "Forest", "Ocean" or "Berry".

**Animations** – How much the app celebrates:

- *Full* shows confetti and a card for every new species.
- *Reduced* shows only the confetti.
- *Off* shows only the numbers.

Nothing is lost either way: a new species is still collected and still counts.
Next to it you can switch off the app's **vibration**.

**Announcements** – With **"Announce bird names"** the app announces new birds to you through headphones or the speaker. You can set how much and how often it speaks. Announcements start switched off.

**Location** – The app determines your location by GPS, or you pick your area yourself on the map. That is how it knows which animals occur where you are and how many stars each one is worth. Without a location there are no stars.

**Privacy** – Here you allow, one by one, whether the app may load map images, place names and the weather from the internet. Without this permission, everything else works just the same.

**Backup** – Save your collection in a file (see below).

### Advanced settings

Here you find settings for sound, detection, the spectrogram, recordings and storage. The most important ones:

**Confidence threshold** – How sure the app has to be before it shows an animal. The default is **35 %**.
A higher value means fewer mistakes, but also fewer birds. Raising the value is always allowed.
**Below 35 % there are no stars** – the app then switches to test mode and asks you first.

**Species filter** – Makes sure the app only recognizes animals that can occur where you are. If it is off, there are no stars.

**Keep listening with the screen off** – The app keeps listening while the phone is in your pocket and stops by itself after 15 to 120 minutes. More under [Live](../listening/).

**Save recordings** – For every find, the app saves a short sound recording that you can listen to in the journal. This starts switched on.

**Storage** – Shows how much room the recordings and the collection take up. The app tidies away old recordings automatically: only once they are older than **30 days**, and only when a species has more than **100 recordings**. You can change both values. **"Delete recordings"** frees up space all at once – collection, stars and journal stay as they are.

## Backing up your collection

Smartfinch stores everything on your phone and uploads nothing. But that also means: if the phone is lost or reset, the collection is gone.
That is what the **backup** is for.

<NumberedList>

1. **Open Settings → Backup.**
2. **Tap "Save a backup".**

    The app creates a single file with all species, all days and all stars. You decide where it goes – into a cloud or onto a computer, for example.
3. **Include the recordings?**

    This starts switched off. If you switch it on, the recordings are back on a new device too – but the file can then grow to several hundred megabytes.
4. **On a new phone:** tap **"Restore from a backup"** and choose the file. Everything on the device is then replaced by the contents of the file.

</NumberedList>

In addition, the app keeps **automatic backups** on the phone – one per day, for the last three days on which something was added.
They help after a crash or a failed update. Against a lost phone, however, only a backup you have saved somewhere else yourself helps.

## For parents and teachers

- **No account, no data in the cloud.** Recognition, collection, journal and recordings stay on the device.
- **No ads, no purchases, no leaderboards.** There is no way to spend money in the app.
- **Location is only stored coarsely** (a 0.1-degree grid) and never leaves the device.
- **Free text stays private.** Place names a child types in never appear on the shared day picture.
- **Links out of the app** exist only on the About page: privacy policy, terms of use, the app's website and source code, and the BirdNET website.
- **No time pressure.** The app has no countdowns, no penalty points and no pushy notifications.
- **The AI can be wrong.** Smartfinch makes no scientific claim. Encourage children to listen closely and, if possible, to see the bird as well.

On first start, the app explains in a few steps what it needs the microphone and the location for, and only then asks for permission.
You can show the introduction again at any time in the advanced settings.
