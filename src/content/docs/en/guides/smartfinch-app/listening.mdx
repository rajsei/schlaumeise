Live is the heart of Smartfinch.
There is only this one mode and only one big button: tap it, and the app starts listening through your phone's microphone.
When the AI recognizes a call or a song, the animal appears on the screen straight away – with the stars you earn for the find.
All of this happens directly on your phone, without any internet.

## Typical uses

Live is especially suited to:

- 10 to 30 minutes at the window, on the balcony or in the garden after school,
- the way to school or a walk in the park,
- a trip to the woods or a lake – often lots of new species are waiting there at once,
- outings with the class or the family.

## What is Live not suited to?

- leaving the phone recording overnight or for days. Each animal earns stars **only once a day**, so long recordings achieve almost nothing.
- recording birds scientifically, for example in a survey with fixed count points or routes. That is what BirdNET Live is for.

## How Live works

<NumberedList>

1. **On the home screen, tap the big "Live" tile.**
2. **Start with the big button in the middle.**

    With this button you start, pause and stop.
3. **Be quiet and hold the phone still.**

    Watch the spectrogram. You often see a call in it before the app recognizes it.
4. **Collect the animals the app recognizes.**

    Every find appears as a card with a name and stars. At the top you see how many stars and species you already have today.
5. **Stop when you are done.**

    The journal then opens and shows you your day with all your finds.

</NumberedList>

## What does the app show while listening?

### The spectrogram – a picture of the sound

The spectrogram runs at the top. It shows what the sound looks like: **high notes at the top, low ones at the bottom**.
Try it out: talk while a bird is singing and watch its call disappear underneath your own voice.
You can find out more about how sound works and what a spectrogram shows in the [knowledge base](../../../knowledge/acoustics/).

### The find cards

Below the spectrogram, all the animals the app recognizes appear. Each card shows:

- the **name** of the animal,
- the **stars** you earn, for example `⭐ +100`,
- a **bonus label** when the find is worth more than usual, for example `NEW ×3` for a species you have never heard before, or `REGULAR ×2`,
- a bar showing **how sure** the AI is.

If you hear an animal again on the same day, the card says **"already collected today ✓"** instead of a number.
And if a find earns no stars, the card says so too, for example **"no stars — not expected here this week"**.

Sometimes the card shows a hint like **"Early! Normally not here before April."** That means the bird isn't usually here yet at this time of year – which is exactly why it is worth so much right now.

Tap a card to find out more about the animal: pictures, a description and when it occurs where you are.

### Your day so far

Right at the top you see what you have collected today, for example **"Today: ⭐ 850 · 9 species"**.

### A new species!

When you find a species for the very first time, there is **confetti** and a small card: **"New species! Added to your collection."**
The card disappears by itself after a few seconds or when you swipe it away.
If the app finds several new species at once, it shows one shared card, for example **"3 new species!"**.

None of this interrupts listening: while you celebrate, the app keeps listening and doesn't miss a bird.

## When the app says "Test mode"

If **"Test mode — no stars, nothing collected"** appears at the top, the app still recognizes animals but awards no stars.
This happens when

- the **species filter** is switched off in the advanced settings,
- the **confidence threshold** has been lowered below 35 %, or
- the app has **no location**.

The app tells you the reason and takes you straight to the right setting with a button.
Everything you hear in the meantime stays in your journal – just without stars.

If it says **"No stars right now"**, the app couldn't work out at the start which animals live where you are.
Stopping and starting again usually helps.

## Keep listening with the screen off

On the way to school or on a hike, the phone should be able to stay in your pocket.
For this there is the switch **"Keep listening with the screen off"** in the **advanced settings**. It starts switched off.

- When it is on, the app keeps listening while the phone is locked or another app is open.
- After **15, 30, 60 or 120 minutes** away from the app it stops by itself (default: 30 minutes).
- On Android, a **notification** shows that the app is listening. You can also stop it there. For this to work, you need to allow the app to show notifications.
- New species you found with the screen off are celebrated as soon as you come back to the app – all of them together on one card.

The first time you leave the app while listening, it asks you once whether it should keep listening next time.

## Having bird names announced

The app can also **announce** new birds to you. Then you can keep looking into the trees instead of at the screen.
To do so, switch on **"Announce bird names"** under **Announcements** in the settings.
It is best to use headphones so that the microphone doesn't pick up the announcement itself.
